# 📊 Key Insights

- Peak energy usage occurs between 7 PM – 10 PM
- Lowest usage occurs during late night hours (2 AM – 5 AM)
- Strong seasonal trends observed in monthly consumption
- Data distribution is right-skewed with occasional spikes
- Energy usage is predictable based on time and past behavior


## 1. Daily Trend Insight
From first graph:

Power consumption shows cyclical patterns over time
There are frequent spikes and drops, indicating varying daily usage
No strong upward trend → usage is relatively stable over years


## 2. Monthly Trend Insight
From monthly graph:

Clear seasonal pattern observed
Higher consumption at start/end of year → likely winter months
Lower usage mid-year → likely summer or reduced heating usage

### 💡 Insight:
Energy consumption is influenced by seasonal factors such as weather conditions.


## 3. Hourly Usage Insight
From hourly bar chart:

Lowest usage: late night (2 AM – 5 AM)
Gradual increase in morning
Peak usage: 7 PM – 10 PM

### 💡 Insight:
Peak electricity demand occurs during evening hours when household activity is highest.


## 4. Distribution Insight
From histogram:

Data is right-skewed
Most values are concentrated at lower consumption levels
Few extreme high values (outliers)

### 💡 Insight:
Power consumption is generally low to moderate, with occasional high spikes.


## 5. Correlation Insight
From heatmap:

Global_active_power strongly correlates with:
Global_intensity
Sub-metering features

### 💡 Insight:
Key electrical parameters are highly interdependent, which is useful for predictive modeling.


## 6. Behavioral Insight
Combining everything:

Users:
Consume less power at night
Peak usage in evening
Show seasonal dependence

### 💡 Final Business Insight:
Energy demand is predictable based on time and season, making it suitable for forecasting models.
